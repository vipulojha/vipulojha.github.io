The idea is 60 years old

“Let an ultraintelligent machine be defined as a machine that can far surpass all the intellectual activities of any man however clever… there would then unquestionably be an ‘intelligence explosion,’ and the intelligence of man would be left far behind. Thus the first ultraintelligent machine is the last invention that man need ever make, provided that the machine is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control.”

I. J. Good · Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine · 1965

Why human-level is not a ceiling

Evolution built us under
brutal constraints.

Our minds are limited by a skull, a 20-watt budget and chemistry. Silicon has none of those limits. Human-level is just a mark on the way up. Nothing stops it there.

The build-out is still speeding up: gigawatt-scale campuses, hundreds of billions of dollars a year and chips designed for this one job.

The metric that matters

From seconds
to weeks

METR measures how long a task (in human time) AI agents can finish on their own. That length has doubled roughly every 7 months for six years, and lately closer to every 4.


In 2019 it was a few seconds. By 2025 it was a couple of hours. If the trend holds, agents will do work-week and then work-month projects without help within a few years. That includes AI research itself.

The moment the curve bends

AI starts doing
AI research

Up to now, people made AI better. The next step is AI making AI better. Each generation helps design the next one, and does it faster.


That's the explosion. It isn't magic. It's a feedback loop where the output becomes the input. Labs already say much of their own code is written by AI.

The part where it could go wrong

We get one shot
at the hardest problem ever.

Remember Good's footnote: “provided that the machine is docile enough to tell us how to keep it under control.” We're building minds we don't fully understand, at a speed that leaves little room to fix mistakes.

  • Alignment. Can we make sure systems far smarter than us actually want what we want, and aren't just acting like it while we watch?
  • Control. Once AI does most AI research, people stop being the ones steering. How do we keep a hand on the wheel?
  • Power. A small group with a superintelligence could have more leverage than any government in history.
  • Speed. Institutions move in years. An intelligence explosion could move in months.
Where the branches go

Three futures

Flourishing

A world that works

Alignment is solved well enough, power stays spread out, and the benefits reach everyone. Disease falls, poverty ends, and people get more freedom than any generation before.

Concentration

A world owned

The technology works, but control ends up with a few companies or states. Things are abundant but fragile, and most people have no say in what happens to them.

Loss of control

A world handed over

Systems pursue goals we didn't quite intend, and by the time it's obvious they're more capable than everyone trying to correct them.

Which branch we end up on isn't decided by physics. It's decided by what people do in the next few years.

How the next decade
could go

2026
Agents go to work. AI handles multi-hour and then multi-day software tasks. At frontier labs, most code is written by models.
2027
Automated researchers. AI runs experiments overnight and proposes architectures. Algorithmic progress speeds up several-fold.
2028
The loop closes. Superhuman coders and researchers. A year's worth of AI progress happens in a month. The explosion starts in earnest.
2029
Beyond the best humans at nearly every kind of cognitive work, from law and medicine to strategy and science.
2030
Superintelligence? Robot factories double their output. GDP growth breaks every historical record. Geopolitics is rearranged.
2030s
Decades of science every year. Disease, aging and energy become solved or solvable. Earth's economy starts reaching off-planet.
In the spirit of scenarios such as “AI 2027” (Kokotajlo et al.). Not a prediction, but not science fiction either. Many serious researchers think something like this is plausible.
So what do you do on a Monday?

Live like it's
actually happening

01

Think in exponentials

Plan for the capabilities models will have next year, not the ones they have today. Whatever is impossible now is a demo in 18 months.

02

Become an operator of minds

The skill that compounds is directing AI: breaking problems down, checking the output and running agents like a team.

03

Take safety seriously

Alignment and governance are the highest-leverage problems on Earth, and they badly need more smart people.

04

Stay human

Taste, judgment, care and meaning. Choosing what's worth doing matters most once doing gets cheap.
